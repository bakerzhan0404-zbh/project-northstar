# JPMorgan Public Benchmark Pack

Use this pack to frame questions, test ACG's maturity, and strengthen your executive narrative. Do not use any benchmark as a substitute for client analysis.

All figures below were visible on official JPMorgan pages when this pack was reviewed on 30 July 2026. Re-open the linked source, review its footnotes, and record your verification in `source_log.csv` before placing a figure in a deliverable.

## Benchmark 1 — Automation adoption exceeds automation maturity

J.P. Morgan's 2026 Payments Outlook reports that:

- **87%** of organizations have some level of automation in treasury and payments infrastructure.
- Only **39%** describe their systems as mostly or fully automated.

**Official source:** [Payments Outlook: Five Trends Powering Payments in 2026](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/payments/trends-innovation/payments-outlook-trends-2026)

**How to use it:** Distinguish the presence of automation from end-to-end automation. Ask whether ACG's automated bank connections are undermined by manual intake, inconsistent reference data, or spreadsheet consolidation.

**Do not conclude:** ACG should target either percentage, or that automation alone creates value.

## Benchmark 2 — Treasury professionals value embedded connectivity

The same report states that **92% of treasury professionals** view embedded banking and finance capabilities as very or somewhat valuable.

**Official source:** [Payments Outlook: Five Trends Powering Payments in 2026](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/payments/trends-innovation/payments-outlook-trends-2026)

**How to use it:** Explain why integration between banking, ERP, and treasury workflows is an industry priority. Then determine whether ACG has the data ownership and process consistency required to benefit.

**Do not conclude:** An embedded solution is automatically the correct Wave 1 investment.

## Benchmark 3 — Global scale drives account complexity

J.P. Morgan's public Fortescue case describes a treasury network of **more than 180 bank accounts** across multiple countries and currencies as the company expanded globally.

**Official source:** [How J.P. Morgan Payments facilitated Fortescue's next-gen treasury transformation](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/treasury/liquidity-management/fortescue-global-treasury-transformation)

**How to use it:** Compare the structural drivers of complexity—growth, markets, currencies, legal entities, and business requirements—not the raw account count alone.

**Do not conclude:** ACG's 55-account footprint is efficient merely because it is smaller, or inefficient merely because it is fragmented.

## Benchmark 4 — Structured payment data supports automation

J.P. Morgan explains that ISO 20022 uses structured, rich data and can support better reconciliation, screening, automation, and cross-border processing. JPMorgan Chase states that it went live with the standard in **March 2023**.

**Official source:** [ISO 20022 Migration: The journey to faster payments automation](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/treasury/treasury-management/what-is-iso-20022)

**How to use it:** Connect structured data to ACG's incomplete beneficiary and invoice information, exception repair, and reconciliation concerns.

**Do not conclude:** Northstar should become a detailed ISO 20022 migration project.

## Benchmark 5 — Payments operates at global infrastructure scale

J.P. Morgan's public payments-solutions page states that it processes **more than $10 trillion in payments daily**, across **120 currencies**, **160 countries**, and **40 payment methods**. The page also states that **90% of the Fortune 500** choose J.P. Morgan for payment needs.

**Official source:** [Payments Solutions](https://www.jpmorgan.com/payments/solutions/treasury/payments)

**How to use it:** One context statement can demonstrate why treasury design must accommodate scale, formats, currencies, controls, and resilience.

**Do not conclude:** These figures quantify ACG's benefit or establish product fit.
