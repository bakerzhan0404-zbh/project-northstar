# Project Northstar — Personal Reflection

**Prepared by:** Baker · **Date:** 24 August 2026

## 1. Which initial hypothesis changed most, and why?

The biggest change was from “ACG likely has a substantial cash-release opportunity” to “ACG first has a cash-evidence and decision-right problem.” The balance analysis showed large positive positions and simultaneous deficits, but the data could not establish authoritative balance type, operational buffers, local restrictions, complete cash needs, transferability, timing, economics, or authority. The result was not to discard the liquidity opportunity; it was to redesign the recommendation around certification and controlled decisions before value.

## 2. Which analysis most influenced the recommendation?

The visibility-method analysis and the option gate-then-score model mattered most together. Delays were concentrated in Portal and Spreadsheet methods, while API and host-to-host observations were same-day under the supplied proxy. That made the root issue specific enough to address. The option model then showed that a federated spine could correct enterprise ownership and data/control gaps without removing local rights. The sensitivity work was important because the preferred option survived five plausible weight changes.

## 3. Where did I balance value, risk, feasibility, and control?

The hardest balance was refusing to turn screened liquidity, estimated fees, or management-estimated hours into a “total benefit” while still giving management an actionable plan. I separated cash, P&L, capacity, and risk; kept recognized value at zero; introduced a provisional cost range only as an analyst assumption; and used stage gates so evidence could change the direction. This reduced rhetorical impact but improved decision quality.

## 4. Which claim remains least certain?

Future realized value is least certain. The base `$35m` liquidity screen, four candidate accounts, 150 hours/month, and `$1.155m` one-time cost are useful planning anchors, but none is a validated return. The program could create substantial operational control and decision speed with modest recognized financial value, or later evidence could support a stronger case. The present work cannot responsibly choose between those outcomes.

## 5. What would I do next with additional client access?

I would observe two daily cash-position cycles, link receipt timestamps and balance types to the 55-account population, and complete account-level mobility/buffer interviews with Regional Finance and Legal/Tax. I would also retrieve the authoritative payment population and event/status lineage, review the 120 purposive records with requestors and repair teams, obtain vendor/internal cost evidence for CR01–CR10, and rehearse the rollback/control design with IT and Internal Audit observing.

## 6. What consulting behavior will I carry forward?

I will keep asking what decision a number can actually support. The most valuable discipline in this project was not producing an estimate; it was defining the boundary between a screen, a hypothesis, a validated fact, a funded target, and a recognized outcome. I will also carry forward the habit of naming the strongest counterargument, building switch conditions before implementation, and treating local stakeholders' operating constraints as design inputs rather than resistance to be managed away.

## Closing reflection

My answer became narrower and more useful over four weeks. I began with a transformation problem and ended with a controlled decision system: a federated direction, an explicit fallback, named evidence owners, non-compensating gates, and a 90-day path to the next decision. The project reinforced that credibility comes less from the size of the opportunity than from showing precisely what is known, what is assumed, and what management must prove next.
