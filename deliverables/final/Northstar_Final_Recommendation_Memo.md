## Decision requested

Authorize a **90-day evidence mobilization** for a federated treasury operating model, subject to five conditions:

1. Group Treasury owns the enterprise data, policy, control, and performance spine; regional and local teams retain approved execution and emergency rights.
2. Named owners complete the account, payment, mobility, control, service, architecture, cost, and benefit evidence packages before any production go-live.
3. The FY2026 `$1.0–$1.5m` amount remains an initial-stage ceiling—not a budget approval, spend authority, committed funding, cost estimate, or ROI denominator.
4. Day 90 is a separate `stop / extend / bounded-pilot` decision. It does not authorize production change, cash movement, account closure, labor removal, procurement, benefit recognition, or scale.
5. If global ownership, minimum integration readiness, affordability, or a critical control/service/local-right/rollback gate fails, ACG switches to **local stabilization** while preserving the common data and control minimum.

The Steering Committee should also confirm the seven accountable initiative owners, the exact North America eight-week Q4 freeze calendar, and Rachel Kim's required approval path for any North America production change.

## Executive recommendation

ACG should pursue **federated coordination**, not immediate global centralization and not indefinite local remediation. The evidence shows an enterprise problem in visibility, payment operations, ownership, and control, but it does not yet prove transferable cash, realized savings, or implementation returns. A 90-day evidence mobilization is the smallest defensible commitment: it builds the authoritative populations, owner decisions, control evidence, bottom-up costs, and reversible pilot readiness needed for a later investment decision. The design preserves local responsiveness and emergency rights, works around the three ERP environments, and stops value from being booked before Finance can trace it.

## What the evidence shows

### 1. ACG lacks a reliable daily enterprise cash view

- **Fact:** 5,792 of 9,955 supplied account-days were reported on the same calendar date, a `58.18%` proxy rate. Twenty-three of 55 accounts use Portal or Spreadsheet reporting; those methods account for all delayed observations in the supplied pattern.
- **Implication:** The first management decision is authoritative source, timestamp, definition, owner, reconciliation, and exception handling—not cash movement.
- **Boundary:** The calculation is a calendar-date proxy, not start-of-day visibility or elapsed-24-hour performance.

### 2. Liquidity screens identify a validation agenda, not movable cash

- **Fact:** The 14-day `$21m` screen passes 168/168 complete windows; `$35m` passes 138/168; `$46.2m` passes 0/168.
- **Implication:** ACG should build account-level mobility and buffer certification before using the screen in a funding decision.
- **Boundary:** Positive or screened cash is not automatically unrestricted, transferable, operationally surplus, or economically beneficial. Recognized cash value remains `$0`.

### 3. Payment friction is material enough to diagnose and too weakly evidenced to automate broadly

- **Fact:** Within the supplied 7,600 records, manual touch is `31.51%`, exceptions `6.30%`, late release `5.00%`, and cross-border activity `10.34%`. Manual-touch records show higher exception and late-release rates than non-manual records, but association does not establish cause.
- **Implication:** Standardize intake, reason codes, cutoff discipline, and event evidence around the highest-friction cohorts before selecting an intervention.
- **Boundary:** The extract is not a certified enterprise population or settlement record. The 120-record Week 3 frame is purposive root-cause evidence, not prevalence or a benefit sample.

### 4. The value case remains a governed hypothesis

- **Fact:** Four accounts meet the narrow dormant/legacy/zero-supplied-payment screen, with `$7,800/year` estimated fees. The management-estimated process file totals `617.72` manual hours/month, while two repair estimates are independent and unreconciled. Diagnostic scenarios retain `$21m / $35m / $46.2m`, `2 / 4 / 4` candidates, and `50 / 150 / 150` productive hours/month.
- **Implication:** Cash, annual P&L, productive capacity, and risk must remain separate and must be admitted to the benefit ledger only after their distinct evidence gates close.
- **Boundary:** Validated, funded, and recognized cash/P&L/capacity value remains `$0`; risk exposure and value are not quantified.

### 5. Affordability is plausible, not demonstrated

- **Fact:** The provisional analyst-assumption range is `$755k / $1.155m / $1.715m` one-time and `$175k / $281k / $442k` recurring per year. The base one-time estimate fits the disclosed ceiling; the high case exceeds it by `$215k`.
- **Implication:** Close CR01–CR10 with quotes, estimates, internal capacity, timing, and run costs before funding. A cost above `$1.5m` returns for staged approval.
- **Boundary:** These are planning assumptions, not vendor evidence. ROI, NPV, and payback remain unavailable because both cost evidence and recognized benefits are incomplete.

## Recommended operating model and initiative portfolio

Group Treasury should own global policy, the account/data contract, daily enterprise position, mobility standards, and performance. Regional Finance should own local attestation, service context, and governed execution. Shared Services should own controlled payment intake, file execution, reason-coded exceptions, and root-cause action. The CIO should enable staged connectivity, access, audit trail, cybersecurity, resilience, and support across the existing ERP estate. Finance should control the cost and benefit ledger. Local/BU Finance should retain approved critical-payment and emergency rights.

Seven initiatives implement this model:

1. Cash data and visibility foundation.
2. Liquidity certification and funding discipline.
3. Payment intake, controls, and exception reduction.
4. Account rationalization validation.
5. Staged integration, access, and resilience.
6. Governance, service, and adoption.
7. Benefits, cost, and KPI assurance.

The first 90 days emphasize evidence, definitions, owners, safe design, costs, and rollback—not production automation. Wave 1 (Months 4–6) begins only after separate approvals and outside the applicable North America freeze.

## Implementation and governance

The program uses seven non-compensating gates:

- **Day 0 — G0:** authorize evidence mobilization, owners, ceiling treatment, and local fallback.
- **Day 30 — G1:** approve reconciled populations, metric contracts, owners, sources, and calendars.
- **Day 60 — G2:** approve local-right, control, architecture, resilience, and cost evidence or switch/narrow.
- **Day 90 — G3:** decide `stop / extend / authorize a separate bounded pilot` after baseline, cohort, control, service, affordability, and rollback evidence.
- **Months 4–6 — G4:** production go/no-go, with separate funding and North America freeze/sign-off controls.
- **Months 9–12 — G5:** scale only after like-for-like sustained results and Finance-validated value.
- **Months 15–18 — G6:** transfer stable KPI, support, control, risk, and benefit ownership to business as usual.

The CFO-sponsored Steering Committee meets monthly and owns funding, scope, unresolved cross-functional decisions, and stop/switch/scale choices. A weekly Transformation Office integrates workstreams and maintains the gate, issue, change, risk, decision, and benefit records. Workstream owners operate weekly delivery forums. After G6, the Treasury Operating Council owns BAU performance and policy changes.
