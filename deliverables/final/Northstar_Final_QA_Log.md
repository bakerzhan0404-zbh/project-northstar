# Project Northstar — Steering Committee Q&A Preparation Log

**Prepared by:** Baker · **Date:** 24 August 2026
**Use:** Direct answers for a 15-minute Q&A; distinguish fact, assumption, judgment, and open decision

## 1. What exactly are you asking us to approve?

Approve a 90-day evidence mobilization for the federated direction, seven named initiative owners, the stage-gate model, and the local-stabilization fallback. Do not approve production change, cash movement, account closure, procurement, labor action, benefit recognition, or scale. Day 90 returns with a separate `stop / extend / bounded-pilot` decision.

**Evidence:** Option result `87 / 72 / 60`; all execution gates open.
**Weakest point:** Owners and timing are proposed until management confirms them.
**If challenged:** The approval is intentionally narrow because the investment evidence is not yet complete.

## 2. Why federated coordination instead of local remediation?

Local stabilization can fix selected practices but cannot create the enterprise ownership, daily position, common controls, and acquisition-ready standards the CFO needs. Federated coordination adds that spine while retaining approved local execution and emergency rights. It leads all five plausible option-weight sensitivities.

**Counterargument:** Local work is faster and less disruptive.
**Response:** Local stabilization is the explicit fallback if ownership, integration readiness, or affordability fails; the common data/control minimum is retained either way.

## 3. Why not centralize globally now?

The evidence is not ready. A globally coordinated model increases dependency on legal/local feasibility, architecture, cyber, resilience, cost, service, and rollback evidence that remains open. It also conflicts with the client's requirement to avoid technology-led design and with the three-ERP constraint.

**Falsifier:** If those gates close and scale benefits materially dominate, the Steering Committee can reconsider global coordination at G5 or a separate decision.

## 4. How much cash can we mobilize within 24 hours?

The current answer is **not established**. The supplied data supports 14-day screens: `$21m` passes 168/168 windows, `$35m` passes 138/168, and `$46.2m` passes none. Those figures do not prove authoritative balance, restriction, operating buffer, local need, transferability, timing, economics, or authority.

**Safe message:** Recognized movable cash is `$0` until VG01–VG05 close.
**Next evidence:** Account-level certification and actual funding counterfactual.

## 5. What value do we get for the money?

Today the model shows opportunity hypotheses, not recognized value. Four candidate accounts carry `$7,800/year` estimated fees; the base capacity hypothesis is 150 hours/month; cash screens are separate; risk is unquantified. One-time planning cost is `$755k / $1.155m / $1.715m`; recurring is `$175k / $281k / $442k` per year. ROI, NPV, and payback are unavailable until cost evidence and benefit recognition inputs exist.

**Counterargument:** A decision without ROI is incomplete.
**Response:** That is why the requested decision is 90-day evidence mobilization, not investment approval. G2 requires bottom-up cost evidence and G3 returns with a later decision case.

## 6. Is account rationalization worth doing for only $7,800?

It is a bounded validation and control cleanup, not the core economic thesis. Four accounts meet a narrow screen. Each requires local dependency, continuity, and cost validation, and the fee estimate becomes P&L only after actual removal and Finance approval. The initiative ranks seventh because its value is modest even though it can move quickly.

## 7. Are payment exceptions actually caused by manual work?

Not yet. Manual-touch records have higher supplied exception and late-release rates, but the extract establishes association only. The 120-record frame is designed to link source documents, reason codes, criticality, approvals, event timing, and controls before selecting an intervention.

**Falsifier:** If causes are not consistent, remediable, or safely comparable, stop or redesign rather than automate a symptom.

## 8. How do you prevent centralization from slowing critical payments?

The design retains BU/local critical-payment and emergency rights under approved authority, SoD, rationale, compensating controls, confirmation, and post-event review. It also requires service thresholds, blackout rules, the North America freeze, monitoring, contingency, and ≤4-hour rollback. A service or control failure stops the affected change.

## 9. What happens during the North America Q4 freeze?

Data cleanup, design, testing, evidence review, and low-risk account validation can continue. No North America payment-routing or approval-workflow production change occurs during the confirmed eight-week peak window. The exact dates must close at G1, and any North America production change requires Rachel Kim's sign-off. Until then, Month 5 is the earliest realistic planning assumption.

## 10. What could make the recommendation change?

Switch to local stabilization if ACG cannot establish global data/control ownership, minimum integration readiness, or an affordable initial stage, or if a critical control, service, local-right, resilience, or rollback condition fails. Hold global coordination until its stronger dependencies close and scale/value clearly dominate.

## 11. Which assumption is weakest?

The least certain material claim is the future realized value. Cash screens lack mobility evidence; cost ranges lack quotes; capacity lacks observed removal and redeployment; payment causes lack source linkage. The roadmap is designed to close these gaps without spending or scaling on assumed benefits.

## 12. Why should we trust the numbers?

Material baselines reconcile to source files and are reproduced by Python tests. Evidence labels distinguish supplied data, calculations, assumptions, and judgment. The value ledger fails closed at zero recognized value. Limitations remain on the same page as the number, and model-control passes are never treated as client evidence.

## 13. What are the first three management actions?

1. Confirm the federated direction, fallback, named owners, and no-execution boundary.
2. Reconcile the 55-account and 7,600-record populations and approve the metric/source/owner contracts by Day 30.

## 14. What would failure look like after 90 days?

Failure is not “no production launch.” Failure is unresolved ownership, uncontrolled populations, absent cost evidence, or critical gate gaps without owners and dates. A disciplined `extend` or `switch to local stabilization` decision can be a successful outcome if the evidence says federated execution is not yet viable.

## 15. What decision must not be deferred?

Ownership. Without accountable owners for data, mobility, payments, architecture/control, service, and benefits, ACG cannot close any later gate. Platform selection and global scale can wait; decision rights cannot.
