## Week 3 decision objective

Develop a transparent strategic direction, future-state operating model, two bounded validation designs, and a business-case validation framework that remain credible when the cash, closure, and capacity hypotheses are reduced. The CFO checkpoint should align a direction and its conditions—not approve execution or book unvalidated value.

## Scope and non-negotiable boundaries

**In scope:** eight observable design principles; local-stabilization, federated-coordination, and globally coordinated options; hard gates and weighted sensitivities; an initial stage constrained to the $1.0–$1.5m FY2026 envelope unless separately approved; proposed cash-positioning/payment operating model; RACI and control inventory; visibility and payment pilot charters; four separate value ledgers; ten-slide interim source deck; five CFO answers; updated logs and registers.

**Out of scope:** pilot execution or production change; bank, vendor, or product selection; cash movement; account closure; ERP replacement or detailed solution architecture; legal, tax, regulatory, sanctions, accounting, or credit advice; funded mobility, P&L, capacity, risk, receivables, or FX value without the named evidence and approval.

**Controlled perimeter:** 55 accounts and 9,955 account-days from 1 January–30 June 2026; 7,600 supplied payment records; 1,810 FX rows used for project translation; and nine management-estimated process activities. New evidence enters only after source/population reconciliation and an analysis-log update.

## Work sequence and decision gates

1. Lock design principles before option scoring.
2. Lock criteria and weights before reviewing option totals.
3. Apply non-compensating data, control, service, local-right, resilience, ownership, and rollback gates before using weighted scores.
4. Design the operating model and pilot charters without implying execution approval.
5. Require the initial stage to fit the $1.0–$1.5m envelope or return for staged approval; the envelope is not a cost estimate or authority to spend.
6. Build four separate ledgers for cash release, annual P&L, capacity, and risk; show missing cost/ramp evidence explicitly.
7. Stress the direction at the `$21m` mobility screen, two closure candidates, and 50 hours/month capacity hypothesis. None becomes funded value under current evidence.
8. Reconcile every executive number to a governed output and carry limitations beside the claim.

## Week 3 exit criteria

- Eight principles have observable tests, owners, tradeoffs, and finding traceability.
- Three options cover organization, governance, process, data, technology, controls, service, and deliberate exclusions.
- Option weights sum to 100%, scores are 1–5 with rationale, sensitivities are reproducible, and switching conditions are explicit.
- A critical-gate failure cannot be averaged away; no option is labeled execution-ready under current evidence.
- Future-state processes, RACI, controls, emergency rights, service levels, and two complete pilot charters are internally consistent.
- Cash release, P&L, capacity, and risk remain separate; all uncertified funded values remain zero.
- Costs, ramp, ROI, and payback remain unavailable rather than invented when source evidence is absent.
- Every option states whether its initial stage can fit the $1.0–$1.5m envelope; absent a validated cost range, execution funding remains unavailable.
- The ten-slide source deck and five CFO answers state the recommendation, counterargument, switching conditions, requests, owners, and due dates.
- Week 1, Week 2, and Week 3 tests pass; generated outputs reproduce; logs and submission index are current.
