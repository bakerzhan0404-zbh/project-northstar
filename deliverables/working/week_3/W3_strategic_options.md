# Week 3 — Strategic Options and Sensitivity Analysis

**Prepared by:** Baker  
**Prepared date:** 18 August 2026  
**Status:** Analyst recommendation for Steering Committee alignment; not execution approval  
**Classification:** Confidential — Project Northstar simulated client material

## Decision requested

Advance **federated coordination** as the preferred direction for detailed design and a 90-day evidence-mobilization plan, retain **local stabilization** as the controlled fallback, and hold **global coordination** until material legal/local, architecture, resilience, cost, and mobility evidence closes. Do not authorize production change, cash movement, closure, labor removal, or funded value through this decision.

## Why the decision is conditional

Week 2 proves where the supplied reporting and payment evidence is concentrated, but it does not prove transferability, causes, removability, cost, or execution readiness. The weighted model therefore compares strategic direction only. Every option remains subject to non-compensating gates, and none is execution-ready under current evidence.

## Three genuinely different options

| Design dimension | Option 1 · Local stabilization | Option 2 · Federated coordination | Option 3 · Globally coordinated model |

| Core choice | Repair local reporting/payment practices and introduce minimum group reporting without changing decision rights materially | Establish one global data/control/KPI spine, with regional/local execution and exception rights inside governed standards | Centralize cash-positioning and payment governance/execution broadly, supported by common integration and stronger global decision rights |
| Organization | Local treasury/finance teams retain primary ownership; small Group Treasury standards role | Group Treasury owns policy, data contract, mobility decisions, and performance; regional/local teams execute and own approved exceptions | Central treasury hub owns most decisions and operations; regional/local teams become service recipients and exception channels |

| Cash-positioning process | Improve file timeliness, reconciliation, and escalation locally | Daily governed position assembled from authoritative sources; regional validation precedes group mobility/funding decisions | Central position and funding decisions across most entities subject to certified constraints |
| Payment process | Local intake/cutoff/format fixes and reason coding | Common intake/data/control standards and shared reason taxonomy; execution remains regional/local where justified | Centralized intake, approval orchestration, execution, exception management, and service model |
| Data | Minimum shared definitions; fragmented lineage may persist | One metric/data contract, named owners, common lineage, and controlled regional exceptions | Common global data platform and master definitions with strong central ownership |

**Gate result:** all three options may be discussed as design directions; none is eligible for execution. The globally coordinated option is held because it relies most heavily on the open architecture, local-right, resilience, and cost gates.

## Criteria and weights locked before scoring

Scores use a 1–5 ordinal rubric: `1 = materially conflicts / unsupported`, `2 = weak`, `3 = conditionally adequate`, `4 = strong with explicit conditions`, `5 = strongest relative design fit with an observable closure path`. The weighted result is `Σ(weight × score) ÷ 5`, producing a 0–100 directional score. It is not a confidence or value score, and a `5` does not prove realized performance.

| Criterion | Weight | What is being judged |
|---|---:|---|
| Evidence and diagnostic fit | 20% | Direct response to F07–F11 without assuming unsupported causes or scope |
| Control, service, and resilience | 20% | Ability to preserve/replace controls, critical service, emergency rights, and rollback |
| Feasibility, speed, and affordability | 20% | Deliverability around current capability, three ERP environments, evidence gaps, and the initial FY2026 envelope |
| Local adaptability | 15% | Ability to respect entity/market requirements and retain governed local action |
| Data/technology scalability | 10% | Reusable ownership, lineage, integration, and acquisition-ready standards |
| Future value-enablement potential | 10% | Plausible path to value after certification, without treating scenarios as benefits |
| Reversibility and learning | 5% | Ability to test, learn, stop, and return to an approved prior process |
| **Total** | **100%** | Weights remain an analyst proposal until Steering Committee confirmation |

## Base weighted matrix

| Option | Evidence fit | Control / service / resilience | Feasibility / speed / affordability | Local adaptability | Scalability | Future value | Reversibility | Weighted score / 100 | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Local stabilization | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 2 | 5 | **72** | Validate as fallback |
| Federated coordination | 5 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | **87** | **Advance detailed design** |
| Globally coordinated | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 5 | 5 | 2 | **60** | Hold pending gates |

### Score rationale

- **Local stabilization** scores well on speed, local autonomy, and near-term reversibility, but it only partly addresses common data ownership, cross-entity coordination, and scalable standards. It remains a credible fallback if global ownership or integration readiness cannot be established.
- **Federated coordination** best fits the source-concentrated evidence: it can standardize the data/control contract and target common friction while retaining regional/local execution and emergency rights. Its score depends on disciplined governance and owner adoption that are not yet confirmed.
- **Globally coordinated** offers the greatest theoretical scale and value path, but current evidence does not support the required centralization, architecture, resilience, local/legal readiness, or cost. Its attractiveness cannot override those gates.

## When the preferred direction changes

| Switching condition | Direction after switch | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Group-wide data/control ownership, a minimum interface plan, or affordable 90-day mobilization cannot be established | Local stabilization | Preserve service and improve local evidence while requiring a minimum controlled group feed; do not abandon DP-01/05/07 |
| Extreme weights of 5/10/40/20/5/5/15 assign 55% to feasibility and reversibility and materially reduce evidence/control/scale/value | Local stabilization (88 versus federated 85) | The model then values near-term bounded delivery more than cross-entity coordination |
| Legal/tax/local rights, architecture, cyber/resilience, cost, mobility, and rollback evidence all close, and weights of 5/10/5/5/45/25/5 assign 70% to scale/value | Globally coordinated (85 versus federated 82) | Only then can theoretical scale lead; current open gates still block execution |
| Federated design fails a critical gate | Remove or redesign federated option | A failed non-compensating gate cannot be averaged away, regardless of weighted score |

## Required downside challenge

The direction is tested at a `$21m` mobility screen, two closure candidates, and 50 hours/month of capacity. These remain unvalidated scenario inputs and contribute **zero** to the funded case today.

Federated coordination still stands as the design direction because its no-regret data ownership, control, visibility, reason-coded payment diagnosis, and staged learning do not require booking those benefits. What does **not** stand is implementation funding, ROI/payback, enterprise scale, or any benefit commitment until costs and evidence gates close.

## Recommendation and next decision

1. **Advance federated coordination for detailed operating-model and pilot design.** This is the best-supported direction, not an execution authorization.
2. **Retain local stabilization as the documented fallback.** Trigger it if ownership, integration readiness, or affordability cannot clear the early gates.
3. **Hold global coordination.** Reconsider only after local/legal, architecture, resilience, economics, and mobility evidence is materially stronger.
4. **Require owners to close the three evidence packages.** Until then, funded mobility, P&L, capacity, and risk value remain zero and costs/ROI remain unavailable.
