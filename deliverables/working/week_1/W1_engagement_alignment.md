# Week 1 — Engagement Alignment

**Prepared by:** Baker  
**Reporting date:** 2 August 2026  
**Evidence status:** Initial framing; hypotheses remain subject to Week 2 validation

## Executive question

Should ACG authorize a 90-day mobilization to redesign global treasury, and if so, which first-wave changes will improve daily cash visibility, liquidity control, payment reliability, and operating efficiency within 12 months without weakening local responsiveness, resilience, or required controls?

## Why the decision matters now

ACG cannot currently provide a reconciled same-day answer to how much cash can be mobilized within 24 hours. A European entity also borrowed while other entities held surplus cash, and a late supplier payment required manual intervention to avoid disruption. These are signals—not yet proof—that fragmented visibility, governance, data, and payment processes may create avoidable cost and control exposure.

## Scope and non-scope

| In scope | Out of scope |
|---|---|
| Bank-account and legal-entity footprint | Product selection and vendor procurement |
| Cash visibility, reporting timeliness, and source quality | Legal, tax, regulatory, sanctions, or accounting advice |
| Ledger, estimated available, restricted, required, and potentially mobilizable cash distinctions | Detailed system architecture or implementation coding |
| Payment execution, exceptions, late release, repair, and diagnostic reconciliation | Bank pricing negotiation or credit underwriting |
| Treasury organization, governance, controls, data ownership, and technology enablement | Claims about proprietary JPMorgan capabilities or actual client recommendations |
| Options, value ranges, sequencing, change risks, and KPIs | Treating positive balances as automatically transferable |

## Kickoff questions

1. What exact decision must the steering committee make, and what evidence would cause it to defer mobilization?
2. What does “reliable daily cash visibility” mean: timestamp, balance type, coverage, confidence, and reconciliation standard?
3. Which legal, tax, regulatory, operational, and business-continuity rules determine whether cash is movable within 24 hours?
4. **Challenge:** What evidence supports the belief that at least ten accounts can close, and which dependencies could make a dormant account essential?
5. **Challenge:** Could the borrowing event have been economically rational because surplus cash was restricted, mistimed, or costly to transfer?
6. **Challenge:** Is technology genuinely the constraint, or would automating inconsistent ownership and reference data scale existing defects?
7. Which payment types, entities, and upstream data defects create the highest operational and supplier risk?
8. Which controls are mandatory, and where does current manual effort represent required control rather than avoidable waste?
9. What local autonomy and service levels must the future model preserve, especially for urgent payments and peak season?
10. Which benefits must be visible inside 12 months, who will validate each baseline, and what funding envelope is plausible?

## Definition of a successful final decision

At the Week 4 steering committee, ACG can state:

1. the chosen transformation ambition and why it is preferred to credible alternatives;
2. the Wave 1 initiatives, owners, funding range, dependencies, and measurable 12-month outcomes;
3. which cash, P&L, capacity, and risk benefits are observed, assumed, or still subject to validation;
4. which local, legal, tax, regulatory, cyber, resilience, and control conditions apply;
5. the first 90-day actions, stage gates, service protections, and decisions deliberately deferred.

## The three questions most likely to determine the recommendation

1. How much cash is demonstrably transferable after restrictions, operating buffers, timing, and entity constraints?
2. Which account and payment-process changes deliver material value without weakening control or business service?
3. Which operating model can ACG implement around three ERPs, limited FY2026 funding, local autonomy, and peak-season constraints?

**Data issue most capable of reversing the recommendation:** the dataset cannot prove legal or operational transferability. If most apparent surplus is restricted or required locally, a liquidity-centralization-led business case could materially weaken and Wave 1 should shift toward visibility, data ownership, controls, and process standardization.
